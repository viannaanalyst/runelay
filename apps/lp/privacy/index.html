Privacy Policy

runelay runs on your own computer or server, and your data stays there. This page explains the few places where anything passes through us, and what happens to it.

Who we are

runelay is an open source (MIT) MCP gateway: one MCP endpoint that gives your AI agents your connected accounts, skills and memories. It is maintained by Anderson Medeiros and the source is at github.com/runelay/runelay. It ships as a Docker image, a CLI and a desktop app.

In this policy, “we” means the maintainer of runelay.

runelay is not a hosted service

Each person runs runelay on their own machine or server. Connected-account tokens, memories, skills and settings are stored in your own instance, in a SQLite database, encrypted at rest with AES-GCM.

We have no access to any instance or to the data in it, and we do not collect it.

How sensitive data is protected

Sensitive data in runelay means the OAuth access and refresh tokens, API keys and credentials of your connected accounts, plus your memories and skills. It is protected like this:

  • Encryption at rest: tokens and credentials are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with a random IV per value and an authentication tag that rejects tampered data. The key is derived from RUNELAY_SECRET (32+ characters), which exists only on your instance and is never sent to us.
  • Encryption in transit: calls to providers and through the OAuth broker use HTTPS (TLS). OAuth sign-ins use PKCE and a state check.
  • Access control: the MCP endpoint and the dashboard require an API key or a signed-in session. Keys are compared in constant time, can be revoked at any time, and their use is recorded in an activity log you can review.
  • Least privilege: each connector asks only for the scopes it needs (for example, Gmail is send-only and Drive only sees files runelay created).
  • Local credential storage: the CLI keeps its key in the operating system keychain.
  • No copies on our side: the OAuth broker has no database and does not log codes or tokens, so a breach on our side cannot expose your data.

Vulnerabilities can be reported privately, as described in the Contact section below.

The OAuth broker at auth.runelay.com

The only part of runelay we host is the OAuth broker at https://auth.runelay.com, a Cloudflare Worker whose source is in apps/oauth-broker of the repository. It exists because some providers, like Google, do not allow dynamic client registration. It does two things:

  • It relays the OAuth redirect (the authorization code and state) back to your own instance.
  • It adds runelay’s client secret to token exchange and refresh requests and forwards them to the provider.

It has no database and stores nothing. It does not log authorization codes or tokens. Tokens only pass through it in transit, over HTTPS, and go straight back to your instance. PKCE makes a relayed code useless to anyone but your instance.

You can run your own broker and point your instance at it with RUNELAY_OAUTH_BROKER_URL.

Google user data

Each Google service is a separate connector with its own sign-in, and asks only for its own scopes (plus openid, email and profile to show which account is connected). Your instance calls Google’s REST APIs directly:

  • Google Calendar (calendar, calendar.events): list your calendars and events, create and delete events.
  • Gmail (gmail.send): send email only. runelay cannot read, search or delete your mail.
  • Google Drive (drive.file): create files and list the files runelay created. It cannot see the rest of your Drive.
  • Google Docs (documents, drive.file): create, read and add text to documents.
  • Google Sheets (spreadsheets, drive.file): create spreadsheets, read and write cells.
  • Google Slides (presentations, drive.file): create, read and add slides to presentations.
  • Google Forms (forms.body, drive.file): create forms and add questions.
  • Google Meet (meetings.space.created): create a meeting space and return its link.

That data is accessed only by your own instance, only when your own agents call those tools, and only to provide the features you asked for.

Who receives it. When one of your agents calls a Google tool, the result goes only to that agent, the AI client you connected yourself (for example Claude, ChatGPT or Codex), and that client handles it under its own terms. runelay does not share, transfer or disclose Google user data to anyone else, and it is never transferred to us.

What we never do. Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing features described above. It is never sold, never transferred to data brokers or information resellers, never used for advertising of any kind (targeted, personalized, retargeted or interest-based), never used to determine credit-worthiness or for lending, and never used to build databases unrelated to those features.

AI and machine learning. runelay does not use Google Workspace APIs or any Google user data to develop, improve or train generalized or non-personalized AI and/or ML models, and does not transfer that data to anyone for that purpose.

runelay’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Data retention and deletion

  • Google content (events, emails, documents, files, spreadsheets, slides, forms): fetched on demand when your agent calls a tool, handed to that agent and not stored by runelay.
  • OAuth tokens and the connected account’s email and name: kept encrypted on your instance only while the connection exists, and deleted immediately when you remove the connection.
  • Activity log: records only metadata (tool name, success, duration, time, and for HTTP requests the method, path, status and IP), never the content of your data. It stays on your instance until you delete it.
  • Memories and skills: kept until you delete them.
  • OAuth broker: retains nothing.

To revoke access, remove the connection in runelay, or revoke it at myaccount.google.com/permissions. To delete your data, remove the connection, delete the memories or skills, or delete your instance and its database. Because everything lives on your instance, you delete it yourself and nothing remains on our side. If you need help, ask through GitHub issues.
